## Corrections by area

**United Kingdom**
- Skilled Worker visa: since 22 July 2025 the threshold is £41,700, not £38,700, and most roles must be at RQF level 6, not level 3. Since 8 January 2026, new applicants need B2 English.
- Section 21 "no-fault" evictions were abolished in England from 1 May 2026 (Renters' Rights Act 2025). Five tenancy records were updated.
- The ETA fee is £16 (since 9 April 2025), not £10.
- BRPs have been replaced by eVisas (three records).

**Germany**
- The 3-year fast-track naturalisation was abolished in October 2025 (two records).
- The statutory health insurance threshold is €77,400 for 2026.
- The EU Blue Card is now in § 18g AufenthG, and its settlement permit is in § 18c(2). The records cited § 18b.
- The small-business VAT threshold is €25,000.

**Australia and New Zealand**
- The super guarantee is 12% (it was listed as 11.5%).
- Subclass 485 durations follow the July 2024 rules.
- Subclass 457 was mislabelled as "Temporary Graduate".
- TSMIT was replaced by the Core Skills Income Threshold of A$76,515.
- Subclass 188 closed on 31 July 2024.
- The NZ Active Investor Plus visa changed on 1 April 2025.
- The AEWV median-wage rule was removed on 10 March 2025.
- NSW and Victorian bond lodgement deadlines are corrected.

**Europe**
- Spain ended its golden visa on 3 April 2025.
- Netherlands: the kennismigrant and 30% ruling thresholds are updated to 2026 values.
- France: a first 10-year resident card now needs B1 French (from 1 January 2026).
- The Swedish personnummer format is corrected.
- Ireland: the CSEP thresholds are €40,904 and €68,911 (from 1 March 2026). Rent Pressure Zones have covered the whole country since June 2025, and a national system replaced them in March 2026 (three records).

**Canada**
- **Moving expenses (serious error).** A move from abroad into Canada is generally not deductible, but the record said newcomers could deduct it.
- **Start-up Visa.** It closed to new applications on 31 December 2025.
- **Ontario rent guideline.** The 2026 guideline is 2.1%; two records said 2.5%.
- **Form RC151.** The Climate Action Incentive and Canada Carbon Rebate ended in 2025, so the form now applies for the GST/HST credit only.
- **Family gifts.** "100% tax-free" overstated the rule. Gifts are generally not taxable, but income earned on them is, and T1135 reporting can apply. The "notarized deed" was described as a legal requirement; it is good practice, not law.
- **Proof of funds.** The amount for one person is CAD 15,263, not 14,690.
- **OHIP waiting period.** The records cited Bill 175, which is unrelated home-care legislation. The wait was removed through amendments to O. Reg. 552.
- **CRS age points.** The description was corrected.
- **PNP nomination.** A nomination does not legally guarantee an ITA.
- **Vancouver school registration.** A Vancouver School Board record cited Ontario law; it follows the BC School Act.

**United States**
- Automatic EAD extensions ended for renewals filed on or after 30 October 2025.
- NYC fare: $3.00, with a $35 weekly cap.
- L-1 dual intent comes from INA § 214(h), not § 214(b).
- "Most states require interest-bearing escrow" for deposits was false (two records).
- ACA premium tax credit eligibility for many lawfully present immigrants narrows from 2027.

**Singapore**
- The Employment Pass minimum is SGD 5,600.
- The S Pass minimum is SGD 3,300, and the levy is SGD 650.

The exact before and after text of every change is in the `audits` table (reviewer `KB audit 2026-11-01 ...`) and in `scripts/kb-corrections-2026-11-01.js`.

## Second round: items that needed a human check

These were checked later the same day. Ten records were corrected (`scripts/kb-corrections-2026-11-01.js`, second-round section):

**Canada**
- **Restoration fee:** $246.25 under the current schedule, not $200, plus the $100 visitor record fee.
- **OHIP address change:** 30 days. The 6-day rule applies only to the driver's licence.
- **Ontario fines:** court fines doubled on 1 July 2026, to $100,000 for individuals and $500,000 for corporations. The LTB's own limit of $50,000 was kept.
- **"WP-EXT" letter:** it does not exist (three records). Proof of maintained status is the expired permit plus the Acknowledgement of Receipt and IRCC's work-authorization letter.
- **Category-based draws:** since 18 February 2026 they need 12 months of experience, which need not be continuous. The record said 6 continuous months.
- **IRCC settlement services:** economic-class permanent residents get 6 years of access from 1 April 2026, and 5 years from 1 April 2027.

**Australia**
- **Biosecurity notices:** 2, 6, 12 or 20 penalty units. 12 units is $3,960; the record said it exceeded $6,200.
- **Bridging Visa B:** AUD 575 from 1 July 2026, up from AUD 190.

**Kept as they were:**
- **Tech Nation:** it was reappointed as the Global Talent digital technology endorsing body in April 2025, so the records naming it are correct. The first-round note saying otherwise was wrong.
- **FSRA insurance premiums:** the ranges are estimates and are labelled as such.

**Also fixed:**
- **Tools:**
  - The UK health and care salary is now £25,000 for national pay-scale roles.
  - The LMIA low-wage cap is 10% of the work location since 26 September 2024 (20% in construction and healthcare), in `lmia-readiness-navigator.js`, `multi-country-gazette-crawler.js` and `answer-composer.js`.
- **Question bank:**
  - Q2034 (moving expenses) had the same error as the KB.
  - Q1782 still presented the Start-up Visa as open.
  - Q2043 said US border device searches need reasonable suspicion. *Alasaad v Mayorkas* (1st Cir. 2021) held basic searches need none.

## Third round: new records and the glossary

**New records:** 16 records were added to `global-records.js` later the same day. Seven of them were corrected:
- **Deutschlandticket:** €63 from January 2026; the record said €49.
- **US commuter benefit (§ 132(f)):** $340 a month in 2026; the record said $315 for 2024–2026.
- **US dependent care FSA:** $7,500 from 2026; the record said $5,000.
- **Australia Child Care Subsidy:** since 5 January 2026 the 3 Day Guarantee gives at least 72 subsidised hours a fortnight. It replaced the Activity Test.
- **London bus and tram fare:** £1.85 from 1 November 2026; the record said £1.75.
- **Dubai Nol card:** a metro trip needs at least AED 7.50; the record said AED 15.
- **US independent-contractor rule:** the DOL proposed rescinding the 2024 rule in February 2026. The record now says the 2024 rule applies until a final rule takes effect.

**Glossary (`settlement-glossary-500.js`):**
- Of the 500 terms, only 16 are hand-written. They were checked, and three of their Spanish, French and Arabic translations had dropped figures from the English (0–90 days, 9-digit, under 18); these are fixed.
- **The other 484 are generated filler.** Each term combines one of 12 category labels with one of 41 concept names, for example "Tenancy & Housing Code: Verification Requirement". Every definition reads "Official statutory guidance regarding <concept> under established <category> frameworks", which gives no information. The translations leave the concept name in English.
- They are served by `/v1/glossary` and advertised in the UI as "500 Terms".

**Glossary rewrite (same day): filler replaced with real content, nothing deleted.** Deleting the 484 entries would have left glossary searches empty, so they were rewritten instead. The new content is in `src/glossary-concepts.js`:
- **Concepts:** each of the 41 concepts (appeal right, legal aid clinic, customs declaration and so on) has a plain-language definition with practical advice, in English, Hindi, Punjabi, Spanish, French and Arabic.
- **Areas:** each of the 12 areas has a sentence saying who handles it in Canada, in all six languages. Examples: the Landlord and Tenant Board or Residential Tenancy Branch for housing, OHIP or MSP for health, CRA for tax, and 9-1-1 / 2-1-1 / 9-8-8 for emergencies.
- **How entries are built:** each entry is the concept definition followed by its area sentence. All 484 ids, categories and acronyms are unchanged, so lookups, search, the API and the "500 Terms" UI keep working.
- **File size:** `settlement-glossary-500.js` shrank from 8,608 to 383 lines.
- **Tests:** `test/settlement-glossary.test.js` checks all 500 ids and six languages, the absence of filler, translated concept names, that old ids still resolve, and English and Hindi search.
- **Still needed:** native-speaker review of the Hindi, Punjabi, Spanish, French and Arabic text.

**Still open:**
- The UK's proposed longer settlement period and the Graduate visa dropping to 18 months from January 2027 are announced plans. The records describe current law.
- All 500 glossary terms need a native-speaker review of their translations.

## Structural problems found

1. **"Approved" does not mean reviewed.** 843 records are marked `approved`, and almost all of them were approved by bulk import scripts, not by an editor. Their `reviewMethod` is not `editorial_review`. The corrections above did not change any approval. Each corrected record says in `reviewBasis` that it is not yet editorially reviewed. Recommendation: route the 56 corrected records, and the high-risk ones above, through `/v1/review`.
2. **The crawler can create the hollow records again.** The 15 rejected "Policy Update" records come from the realtime ingestion. It records "a change was detected" as if it were a fact, and turns any date on the page into an "effective date". Until the ingestor is fixed, new ones will appear after each crawl.
3. **Tool modules repeat some outdated values.** The tools are not KB records and were not changed in this audit:
   - £38,700 and the Section 21 rules: `uk-skilled-worker-ilr-navigator.js`, `pr-matrix-simulator.js`, `corridors-registry.js`, `global-visa-matrix.js` and `multi-country-gazette-crawler.js`.
   - CAD 14,690: `pr-matrix-simulator.js`.
   - The old RC151 title: `newcomer-benefits-calculator.js`.
   - Bill 175: `healthcare-navigator.js` and its test.
   - TSMIT A$73,150: `multi-country-gazette-crawler.js`.
4. **Some text is still unchecked.** The glossary (500 terms in 6 languages) and the 400 question-bank items still needed a fact check, especially their translations and legal verdicts. The English figures and dates in both were checked in the second round; three question-bank items were corrected. The translations are still unchecked.

## How to re-run or undo

- Dry run, which shows anything not yet applied: `node scripts/kb-corrections-2026-11-01.js`
- Apply, which is idempotent and backs up the database first: `node scripts/kb-corrections-2026-11-01.js --apply`
- Undo: the database before the audit is saved at `data/backups/settlement-before-kb-audit-2026-11-01.sqlite`. Individual records can also be rolled back from their audit rows with `rollbackAudit()`.
- `test/kb-audit-2026-11-01.test.js` fails if any of the retired values returns to the seed files.
