Canadian citizenship: are you eligible yet?

You need at least 1,095 days (three years) physically in Canada in the five years before you apply. Days spent in Canada as a temporary resident before becoming a PR count as half days, up to 365. This check counts your days and confirms you filed taxes for at least three of those years.

🛡 Your right to a bank account (Bank Act, section 627.17)

Your right under federal law: Under the Bank Act, you can open a personal bank account at a federally regulated bank (such as RBC, TD, Scotiabank, BMO, CIBC or National Bank) if you can prove your identity. A bank CANNOT refuse you because:
  • You do not have a job or employment income;
  • You do not have Canadian credit history;
  • You have been bankrupt;
  • You cannot deposit money right away.

Acceptable Identity Documents (The "Two-Document Rule"):

Under Bank Act regulations, present either:

Option 1: Two Official Identity Documents
  • Foreign Passport (valid)
  • Work Permit, Study Permit, or COPR (IMM 5292 / 5688)
  • Provincial Driver's Licence or Photo Card
Option 2: One Identity Doc + One Reputable Record
  • Foreign Passport
  • Plus: Canadian utility bill, residential lease, or university student card

What to do if a teller refuses to open an account:

  1. Ask for the Branch Manager immediately and cite "Bank Act Section 627.17 and FCAC Commissioner's Guidance CG-1".
  2. Ask for the refusal in writing, with the reason.
  3. File an immediate complaint with the Financial Consumer Agency of Canada (FCAC) at 1-866-461-3222.

💊 Refilling a prescription from home

What a pharmacist may do: A prescription from another country usually cannot be filled as written. A local pharmacist can check your medicine, suggest the local equivalent and, in some provinces and countries, give a short emergency supply until you see a doctor. What they can do depends on local rules and the medicine.

2
Bring your ID: Take your passport and your permit or COPR to the pharmacy.
3
Ask to speak with the pharmacist: Explain that you are new, that you take this medicine regularly and how many days of it you have left.
4
Book a doctor’s visit: Book a walk-in clinic or virtual care appointment soon, so a local doctor can write a new prescription.

💉 Vaccines required for school in Ontario

Ontario school vaccine rule (ISPA): Under the Immunization of School Pupils Act (R.S.O. 1990, c. I.1), students must have up-to-date vaccine records on file with Public Health. A student without them (and without a valid exemption) can be suspended from school.

Exemptions:

1. Medical Exemption (ISPA s.3(1))

A doctor or nurse practitioner confirms that a vaccine is not safe for your child, or that a blood test shows your child is already immune.

2. Conscience / Religious Belief (ISPA s.3(2))

For conscience or religious reasons: complete your local public health unit’s vaccine education session, then sign the official statement of exemption in front of a commissioner for taking affidavits.

CRITICAL HOUSING CRISIS

🚨 Locked out by your landlord

Your right: In Ontario, a landlord cannot change the locks or remove you without an eviction order from the Landlord and Tenant Board, and only the Sheriff can enforce that order. A landlord who locks you out illegally can be fined.

WHAT TO SAY TO THE POLICE (NON-EMERGENCY LINE, OR 911 IF YOU ARE IN DANGER):
"I am the tenant at [your address]. My landlord changed the locks while I was out, and I have not received an eviction order from the Landlord and Tenant Board. Changing the locks without an order is not allowed under section 24 of Ontario’s Residential Tenancies Act. I am asking for an officer to attend to keep the peace while I try to get back into my home."

IDENTITY THEFT & FINANCIAL SECURITY

🛡 Stolen Social Insurance or Social Security Number

If someone has your Social Insurance Number (SIN) or Social Security Number (SSN), take these four steps in order:

  1. Add a fraud alert to your credit file: Call Equifax Canada at 1-800-465-7166 and TransUnion Canada at 1-800-663-9980. A fraud alert asks lenders to confirm your identity before opening new credit in your name.
  2. Report it to the police: File an identity theft report with your local police and write down the report (occurrence) number.
  3. Tell Service Canada: Visit a Service Canada Centre with your police report, primary ID and proof of address so the misuse is noted on your SIN record. A new SIN is issued only in limited cases where fraud is proven.
  4. Tell the Canada Revenue Agency (CRA): Call 1-800-959-8281 so the CRA can protect your tax account against fraudulent returns and benefit claims.
Canadian Anti-Fraud Centre (CAFC): 1-888-495-8501
CRA individual enquiries: 1-800-959-8281
